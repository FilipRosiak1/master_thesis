Topic: Comparative study of Compatible Substitutions Optimization and Latent Space Optimization in evolutionary design


Description: The thesis investigates the optimization of tree-structured objects of variable complexity, using the evolutionary design of three-dimensional structures as a case study. Genetic representations in such problems are non-positional and the meaning of a genotype fragment depends on its context within the overall structure. The search spaces are discrete and the fitness landscapes are rugged. The thesis will evaluate and compare two complementary approaches to this optimization problem. The first approach will assess the "contextual compatibility" of genotype fragment modifications – specifically, measures that help predict how consistent a given genotype fragment substitution is with the overall structure, syntax, and fitness of a genotype. The second approach will attempt to learn new latent representations that map variable-length genotypes into a fixed-dimensional continuous space, enabling the definition of neighborhood relations and the application of controlled changes within that space. The experimental part will examine how the choice of these measures and representations affects the quality of the best solutions (3D structures) found, the stability of the search process, and the proportion of valid genotypes obtained after modifications or decoding, while accounting for practical constraints such as the size of the training data and syntactic validity checking.
