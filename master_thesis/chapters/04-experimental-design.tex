\chapter{Experimental Design}

\section{Research questions}

The experiments are designed to evaluate how two representation-aware optimization strategies behave when applied to structured, variable-length genotypes. The questions are formulated at the level of the general optimization problem, while the empirical answers are obtained using Framsticks \fone{} genotypes and the \vertpos{} criterion as the common benchmark.

\textbf{RQ1. How reliably do the compared methods produce valid and evaluable genotypes?}

% This question concerns the feasibility and failure behaviour of candidate generation. Both genotype-space substitutions and latent-space decoding may produce candidates that require repair, violate representation constraints, or cannot be evaluated successfully. Validity and evaluability are therefore considered separately from reconstruction quality and final fitness, using only the diagnostic counters available for each method.

\textbf{RQ2. To what extent do the compared methods improve the fitness of starting genotypes?}

% This question evaluates whether CoSO, LSO, and the mini Framsticks EA produce outcomes with higher \vertpos{} values than their recorded starting references. Because the methods use different experimental units and source-handling semantics, improvement is defined relative to the appropriate reference for each protocol and is interpreted together with the final score rather than as a universally interchangeable metric.

\textbf{RQ3. How does explicit context-aware substitution perform in comparison with learned latent-space search?}

% This question compares the two principal representation-aware strategies. The analysis considers optimization outcomes, dependence on configuration, validity-related behaviour, interpretability, and implementation requirements. Since CoSO uses archive-based multi-start runs and LSO uses single-source optimization, their empirical contrast is made at the aggregate and bucket levels rather than treated as a paired per-source experiment.

\textbf{RQ4. How sensitive are the results to starting conditions and initial fitness ranges?}

% This question examines whether the observed final scores and improvement margins depend on the quality of the initial material. Analysing predefined source-fitness buckets distinguishes methods that mainly improve weak starting points from those that can also refine already strong designs, while preserving the method-specific reference used to define improvement.


\section{Compared methods}
\label{sec:Compared methods}

The LSO experiments vary two factors that must be distinguished conceptually. The autoencoder checkpoint defines the learned mapping between an \fone{} grammar derivation and a continuous latent vector, whereas the optimizer defines how vectors in that space are proposed and selected. A method configuration is therefore a fixed checkpoint--optimizer pair rather than an autoencoder alone. The final design does not select a different checkpoint or optimizer for each starting genotype.

The representation-learning stage covered character, grammar-rule, masked grammar, tree-oriented, LHS-conditioned, LHS-depth, Transformer, fitness-aware, and vector-quantized families. The final continuous-space benchmark uses five grammar-rule VAE checkpoints selected from this broader set. TreeVAE-80 and TransformerVAE-90 use 32-dimensional latent spaces and represent two different sequence-modelling architectures. The LHS latent-256 checkpoint uses a masked tree decoder conditioned on the current left-hand-side nonterminal and a 256-dimensional latent space. The LHS-depth checkpoint adds derivation-depth context and uses 128 latent dimensions. The fitness-aware LHS checkpoint also uses 128 dimensions; it was trained with an auxiliary latent fitness-prediction loss, but its auxiliary head is not used as the objective or as a surrogate during final optimization. These entries are fixed learned representations, not five optimization algorithms.

Each checkpoint is combined with three derivative-free latent optimizers. The latent-space EA maintains decoded individuals and latent vectors, uses tournament selection and elitist survival, and proposes offspring through Gaussian mutation, interpolation or extrapolation between parents, directional moves, and occasional random immigrants. CEM instead maintains a diagonal normal distribution and updates it from an elite fraction of each sampled population. CMA-ES adapts a multivariate search distribution, including covariance information, from decoded candidate scores. All three methods use the latent mean of the same source genotype to locate the initial search region and receive true Framsticks \vertpos{} feedback for generated candidates. Their treatment of the source itself differs. Latent EA inserts the original genotype into the initial population with its stored manifest fitness and can retain it through elitist survival. CEM and CMA-ES centre their initial distributions at the encoded mean, decode the sampled vectors (including the current mean), and evaluate those decoded strings; their raw best score does not include an automatic fallback to the original genotype's stored label. Comparative analyses therefore apply the uniform retained-source decision policy defined in Section \ref{sec:Evaluation metrics} rather than treating the three raw outputs as semantically identical.

% ---coso---
The CoSO condition represents the direct genotype-space counterpart to the latent-space methods. It does not use a learned checkpoint and does not optimize an intermediate continuous representation. Instead, it operates on \fone{} strings themselves by extracting coherent tree-based fragments, describing them with representation-level compatibility features, and attempting substitutions between fragments whose descriptors are sufficiently similar. A CoSO method configuration is therefore defined by the fragment-handling procedure, the compatibility thresholds, the archive settings, and the search budget, rather than by a checkpoint--optimizer pair.

In the evaluated CoSO configuration, candidate substitutions are guided by two fragment descriptors: the spatial effect of a fragment and the direction associated with its terminal part. Previously observed fragments are stored in an archive indexed by these descriptors and are later retrieved as possible replacements for fragments in the currently processed genotype. Each proposed replacement is inserted into the full recipient genotype, optionally repaired by Framsticks, and evaluated with the true \vertpos{} criterion. The substitution is accepted only when it improves the current genotype. CoSO is therefore treated as an explicit context-aware substitution method, whose search space remains the original discrete \fone{} representation.

The treatment of the source genotype in CoSO is closer to a direct local-improvement procedure than to the distribution-based latent optimizers. The active genotype is evaluated as a complete \fone{} candidate, then repeatedly modified through accepted substitutions. If no improving substitution is found, the genotype is treated as locally converged, its fragments may contribute to the archive, and the search proceeds from another starting genotype. Consequently, the raw CoSO best score reflects mostly the semantics of the best genotype actually retained during direct search in the original representation.

% ---baseline---
The Framsticks EA provides the single baseline condition used in this thesis. It uses Framsticks mutation and crossover, tournament selection, elitist survival, population and offspring sizes of 100, and 300 generations from each manifest genotype.

\section{Benchmark setup}
\label{sec:Benchmark setup}


The common benchmark is centered on Framsticks \fone{} genotypes and on the \vertpos{} fitness criterion introduced in Section \ref{sec:Dataset and fitness evaluation}. The dataset is used to provide existing genotypes with known starting fitness values, but it is not used as a lookup table for the fitness of newly generated candidates. During optimization, each candidate produced by a baseline evolutionary run or by decoding a latent vector is converted to an \fone{} genotype and evaluated through Framsticks with the same \vertpos{} criterion. Candidates that cannot be represented or evaluated successfully are treated as invalid for the corresponding search procedure.

Starting genotypes for the final benchmark are selected from six initial-fitness buckets. The bucket ranges are \([0.30,0.50)\), \([0.50,0.75)\), \([0.75,1.00)\), \([1.00,1.20)\), \([1.20,1.50)\),

The CoSO evaluation is divided into two experimental stages. The first stage is a compatibility-threshold selection experiment. It uses genotypes sampled from the available \fone{} dataset and evaluates several pairs of spatial and directional compatibility thresholds. The purpose of this stage is not to define a separate final comparator for LSO, but to identify which threshold pair gives the most promising behaviour for the archive-based substitution mechanism. In this experiment, CoSO is analysed as a direct genotype-space optimizer whose performance depends on how restrictive or permissive the fragment-matching criterion is.

The second CoSO stage uses the selected threshold pair in a bucket-aligned benchmark. This stage follows the same initial-fitness bucket structure as the latent-space benchmark: starting genotypes are grouped by their stored source \vertpos{} values so that the method can be analysed separately for weak, medium, and already strong initial candidates. A CoSO run operates directly on \fone{} strings, extracts tree-based genotype fragments, stores previously observed fragments in a compatibility-indexed archive, and tests substitutions in complete genotypes. Newly produced candidates are evaluated through Framsticks with the \vertpos{} criterion.

The final Latent Space Optimization benchmark combines five selected autoencoder checkpoints with three latent-space optimizers. The checkpoint labels used in the benchmark are:

    \item \texttt{10\_transformer\_vae\_epoch90},
    \item \texttt{02\_lhs\_latent256\_seed42\_best},
    \item \texttt{06\_lhs\_depth\_seed42\_best},
    \item \texttt{fitness\_aware\_lhs\_seed42}.
\end{itemize}
For each checkpoint, three latent methods are evaluated: the latent-space evolutionary algorithm, reported as \texttt{latent\_ea}; the Cross-Entropy Method, reported as \texttt{cem}; and CMA-ES, reported as \texttt{cmaes}. The resulting latent part of the benchmark therefore consists of all combinations of five checkpoints, three methods, six buckets, and twenty-five seeds per bucket.

The baseline part of the benchmark uses the Framsticks EA from the same starting genotypes. There is one baseline run for each of the 150 starting genotypes. The nominal search budget is kept consistent at the design level: evolutionary runs use 300 generations with population size 100, while distribution-based latent methods use 300 iterations with the same population size. Actual numbers of unique evaluated genotypes may differ between runs because invalid candidates, duplicates, and cached evaluations are handled by the optimization procedure. The number of unique evaluated genotypes is recorded for all runs, while invalid and duplicate counts are interpreted only for methods and jobs that record them; these measurements are used for later metric analysis rather than being treated as separate benchmark conditions.

The computational execution separates neural latent-space jobs and baseline evolutionary jobs because latent decoding uses trained autoencoder checkpoints, whereas the Framsticks EA baseline does not require neural inference. For the evaluation of generated genotypes, LSO uses the four-file chain: \texttt{eval-allcriteria.sim}, \texttt{deterministic.sim}, \texttt{sample-period-2.sim}.

\section{Evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:Evaluation metrics}

The evaluation uses three connected groups of metrics: representation-learning quality, optimization quality, and search behaviour. This organization reflects the structure of the study. Autoencoder training is not treated only as compression of genotype strings: selected variants also attempt to organize the latent representation with respect to fitness-related information, for example through auxiliary fitness prediction or conditioning. Final optimization quality is nevertheless always measured with the true Framsticks \vertpos{} evaluation, not with a neural prediction.

For autoencoder training and checkpoint selection, the main reconstruction metric is validation exact reconstruction accuracy. A reconstruction is counted as exact when the decoded output string is identical to the corresponding reference genotype string after the representation-specific decoding step. Because exact equality is strict for variable-length structured genotypes, average string similarity is also recorded. Selected checkpoints are additionally characterized by unconditional generation diagnostics: syntactic validity of decoded \fone{} strings, uniqueness, duplicate rate, novelty relative to the available dataset, and, when evaluated, the distribution of generated \vertpos{} values. For fitness-aware variants, fitness-prediction error and correlation are used as auxiliary diagnostics describing whether the learned representation captures fitness-related structure.

The main optimization endpoint is the best \vertpos{} value obtained by a method or configuration. For single-source LSO and baseline runs, the benchmark records a raw best-score field
\[
F^{\mathrm{raw}}_{m,s},
\]
where \(m\) denotes the method or configuration and \(s\) denotes the starting genotype. Because the optimizers handle the source genotype differently, cross-optimizer summaries use a uniform retained-source decision policy:
\[
F^{\mathrm{retained}}_{m,s}
= \max\left(F^{\mathrm{raw}}_{m,s}, F^{\mathrm{source}}_{s}\right),
\]
with retained improvement
\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{retained}}_{\mathrm{source}}(m,s)
= F^{\mathrm{retained}}_{m,s} - F^{\mathrm{source}}_{s}.
\]
This metric corresponds to the practical decision made after optimization: keep the generated result if it improves the starting genotype, otherwise retain the source genotype. It makes latent EA, CEM, CMA-ES, and the baseline comparable under one reported endpoint.

When two single-source methods use the same starting manifest and simulation chain, results are compared directly by starting genotype. For a method or configuration \(m\), the paired difference against the standard Framsticks EA baseline is
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{EA}}(m,s)
= F^{\mathrm{retained}}_{m,s}
- F^{\mathrm{retained}}_{\mathrm{Framsticks\ EA},s}.
\]
Positive values indicate wins against the baseline, negative values indicate losses, and exact zeros indicate ties. Aggregates over starting genotypes include mean, median, maximum, standard deviation, retained improvement, paired difference, and win rate. Since the completed LSO runs and the standard baseline use equivalent four-file simulation chain and the same 150 starting genotypes, this comparison is treated as the main same-condition LSO--baseline comparison.

CoSO is evaluated with compatible quality metrics but a different experimental unit. A CoSO run is an archive-based multi-start search over a genotype pool, not a one-run-per-source trajectory. Its primary endpoint is therefore the best \vertpos{} value found during the run, together with improvement over the best unmodified genotype introduced into that run:
\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{CoSO}}_{\mathrm{subst}}(r)
= F^{\mathrm{best}}_{\mathrm{CoSO},r}
- F^{\mathrm{introduced}}_{r}.
\]
This makes CoSO comparable with LSO via the baselines at the bucket, configuration, and aggregate-result level, while preserving the fact that it is not a paired per-source method.

Search behaviour and stability are described by quantities recorded during each run. The number of unique evaluated genotypes measures how many distinct genotype strings were assigned fitness values under the nominal budget. Invalid-candidate and duplicate-candidate counts describe how much search effort was lost to unusable or repeated candidates, where such fields are recorded. Best-so-far histories over generations, iterations, or evaluation steps are used to show convergence behaviour and stability over time.

Optimization metrics are also analysed conditionally on the initial-fitness buckets defined in Section~\ref{sec:Benchmark setup}. Bucket-conditioned summaries report final scores and improvements separately for weak, medium, and strong starting material. This shows whether a method mainly improves low-fitness candidates, refines already promising ones, or depends strongly on the initial quality of the genotype pool.

\section{Experimental protocol}

Autoencoder training and final optimization are conducted as separate stages. Training uses the grammar-encodable \fone{} data and records the configuration, periodic checkpoints, and reconstruction diagnostics. Validation-enabled runs reserve 20\% of the available examples, giving 2,178 validation items in the recorded runs. Split membership is run-specific: configurations with a fixed training seed use a reproducible split, while identical membership is not assumed for runs whose configuration does not specify a seed. Exact reconstruction and average string similarity are evaluated by deterministic decoding from the latent mean. The retained checkpoint set is then subjected to a separate deterministic reconstruction pass and to unconditional generation diagnostics. These measurements support checkpoint characterization but are not optimization results.

The checkpoint-selection stage produced a frozen list before the final benchmark plan was executed. The ten-checkpoint diagnostic set contains validation-selected checkpoints for the masked and LHS families and explicit epoch checkpoints for TreeVAE-80 and TransformerVAE-90. The final optimization plan fixes five labels: TreeVAE-80, TransformerVAE-90, LHS latent 256, LHS with depth, and the auxiliary fitness-aware LHS model. For every starting genotype, each label is evaluated separately with latent EA, CEM, and CMA-ES. No result-dependent checkpoint switching, optimizer switching, or per-seed portfolio selection is part of a benchmark run.

Starting points are defined by one manifest sampled with pseudorandom seed 20260607. It contains 25 dataset genotypes in each of the six ranges from Section \ref{sec:Benchmark setup}, for 150 source genotypes in total. 
Within the final execution, pseudorandom optimizer states are derived deterministically from a fixed base seed of 777 and the planned configuration and source-seed identifiers. This procedure makes repeated execution of a declared unit reproducible without implying that different algorithms consume identical random-number streams.

Each latent-space EA run uses 300 generations, a population of 100, 100 requested offspring per generation, ten elites, and tournament size three. Its fixed operator settings use mutation standard deviations 0.12, 0.25, 0.50, and 0.90; crossover, extrapolation, directional-move, and random-immigrant probabilities of 0.45, 0.20, 0.25, and 0.05; and no offspring re-encoding in the final plan. CEM and CMA-ES use 300 iterations and populations of 100. CEM uses an elite fraction of 0.25, initial standard deviation 0.35, smoothing 0.35, and minimum standard deviation 0.02; CMA-ES starts with scale 0.35. The Framsticks EA baselines use populations and offspring sets of 100 for 300 generations, ten elites, tournament size three, crossover probability 0.25, and probability 0.25 of mutation after crossover. These settings define one fixed configuration per run.

Initialization follows the method distinction introduced in Section \ref{sec:Compared methods}. Latent EA creates its initial population with the unchanged source genotype as one individual, assigns that individual the stored manifest fitness, associates it with the encoded latent mean, and fills the remaining positions with evaluated decodings of perturbations around that mean. Elitist survival allows the source individual to remain the final raw best. CEM initializes a diagonal normal distribution at the encoded mean, while CMA-ES initializes its mean and covariance-based distribution at the same location. Both distribution methods decode and evaluate their proposed latent vectors, including the current mean; neither inserts the unchanged source string with its stored label into the raw optimizer state. The separately evaluated reconstruction of the source latent mean is also a decoded candidate and must not be confused with the stored source genotype.

The nominal budget is approximately 30,000 population slots per source genotype, in addition to method-specific initialization. This number is not interpreted as an exact count of unique simulator evaluations. Decoding failures, simulator failures, duplicate genotypes, retries, and cached scores affect the number of distinct strings encountered and evaluated. The recorded best-so-far histories use 300 generation or iteration steps when a run completes, and summary rows retain the number of unique scored genotypes together with invalid and duplicate counters where the method explicitly provides them.

Every generated candidate is decoded to normalized \fone{} text and assigned its final objective through Framsticks \vertpos{}; auxiliary predictions are not substituted for this value. Completed artifacts establish two observed simulation configurations for the single-source benchmark. LSO uses \texttt{eval-allcriteria.sim}, followed by \texttt{deterministic.sim}, \texttt{sample-period-2.sim}, and \texttt{only-body.sim}, while baseline uses \texttt{eval-allcriteria-mini.sim}, followed by \texttt{deterministic.sim}, \texttt{sample-period-2.sim}, and \texttt{only-body.sim}. The manifest aligns starting genotypes across these conditions, but aggregation does not convert their generated-candidate scores into a controlled paired comparison.

Final LSO aggregation treats each of the fifteen fixed checkpoint--optimizer configurations as a separate group of 150 runs. For cross-optimizer and bucket-conditioned summaries, every row is transformed by the same post-search decision rule: the reported retained score is the larger of the raw optimizer score and the stored source fitness, and retained improvement is their difference from that source fitness. Source-retention counts are reported alongside this outcome. In latent EA, these counts identify rows whose raw best is already the embedded stored-label source. In CEM and CMA-ES, they identify rows for which the derived policy selects the source because the freshly evaluated raw search output does not improve it; an exact tie changes no numeric value but is still a no-improvement retention decision. Bucket-conditioned summaries contain 25 runs per fixed configuration and bucket.

CoSO is executed as a separate family of direct genotype-space runs because its experimental unit differs from the single-source latent-space benchmark. A CoSO run is an archive-building search over a declared genotype pool rather than an optimization attempt paired with one fixed starting genotype. Each run starts with an empty substitution archive, samples \fone{} genotypes from the declared pool, evaluates candidates with the Framsticks \vertpos{} criterion, and preserves the archive only within that run. When the currently processed genotype reaches local convergence, its fragments may be inserted into the archive and the search proceeds from another genotype sampled from the same pool. No archive state is shared across independent seeds, buckets, or configurations.

The first CoSO stage is a compatibility-threshold selection experiment. It evaluates spatial thresholds

The grid ranges from exact or near-exact compatibility to increasingly permissive archive retrieval, which allows the experiment to test whether CoSO benefits from conservative fragment matching or from broader reuse of archived fragments.
Each configuration is run with 25 independent seeds, a nominal limit of 30,000 within-constraint evaluations (the value arbitrarly set to mimic the population slots), and the available \fone{} genotype dataset as the sampling pool. This stage is used to characterize the sensitivity of the substitution archive to permissive or restrictive compatibility criteria; it is not treated as a paired comparison against individual LSO source-genotype runs.
